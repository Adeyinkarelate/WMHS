ABSTRACT 
maternal health has improved over time, there are still significant gaps in Nigeria's ability to adequately 
monitor maternal health continuously, access health clinicians, record maternal health data, and make 
timely referrals, which can lead to preventable maternal and infant health problems.  The majority of 
current maternal health monitoring is reliant on regular ante natal check -ups, and can be challenging 
to detect new issues that may arise in -between visits.  Current digital health interventions are also 
mostly focused on individual aspects of maternal care rather than on the comprehensive monitoring 
and risk assessment and referral for the entire journey. The present study is thus geared to providing a 
viable and intelligent online platform that can enable continuous monitoring of maternal health 
throughout the pregnancy and postpartum stages.  The study developed/designed, and deployed an AI 
based web-based maternal health monitoring and referral system in Nigeria's healthcare environment. 
It allows pregnant women and new mothers to register, provide symptoms and health details, upload 
test results, view their health history and receive risk assessments and advice. The AI-based risk engine 
evaluates maternal health data and risk indicators to classify women into low risk, medium risk and 
high-risk groups, with a clinician dashboard facilitating risk -based patient monitoring, alerting and 
clinical decision making.  The referral rec ommendation system recommends suitable health care 
facilities based on patient's location, risk and medical needs.  The application's architecture was built 
with the current framework of Next.js, React, TypeScript, PostgreSQL and cloud deployment. Results 
revealed that the AI code achieved an overall accuracy of 92.4% and sensitivity of 94.7% in detecting 
high-risk cases, with a mean response time of 843 milliseconds and a 96.3% completion rate of the 
tasks. The results illustrate the ability of the system t o aid in early risk detection, timely referral, 
ongoing monitoring, and enhancing maternal health care decision making in resource -limited 
environments.

CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCTION 
1.1 Background of the Study 
The issue of maternal health continues to be among the most important public health concerns in 
Nigeria, in line with the country's dedication to caring for the most susceptible of its citizens' citizens. 
Every day, about 810 women die of preventable causes associated with pregnancy and childbirth, most 
of these deaths are reported in low-resource areas in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) (WHO, 2023) Nigeria 
together with the rest of Africa has one of the highest maternal death rates in the world, with a mortality 
rate of 512 d eaths per 100,000 live births (National Population Commission, 2022). The alarming 
statistics highlight the need for creative solutions in monitoring maternal health that can help tackle 
the shortage of healthcare services and the rising demand for good qu ality care. In Nigeria, maternal 
health monitoring is traditionally dependent on regular antenatal visits and regular monitoring. 
Although this episodic approach has been adopted for decades in communities, it has its limitations 
and can be problematic especially in resource-constrained environments (Okafor and Eze, 2023). Life-
threatening problems like pre -eclampsia, gestational diabetes, and fatal distress may occur between 
check-ups, and even if it is not detected, these issues can go into serious troubl e before the next visit 
to the doctor (ACOG, 2022). There is a high workload of healthcare clinicians, with each obstetrician 
handling hundreds of patients and manually sifting through them to identify urgent cases (National 
Population Commission, 2022). T he health manpower shortage is also worsened by the inadequate 
number of skilled health workers, Nigeria has 4.5 doctors per 10,000 population, which is lower than 
the recommended ratio of 23 per 10,000 population by WHO (Federal Ministry of Health, 2022). The 
emergence of digital health technologies, AI, and cloud computing has opened up new possibilities to 
revolutionize the web-based maternal health monitoring and referral system. This predictive capability 
of AI-based applications can now assess clinical parameters, monitor for high-risk patients at runtime, 
and facilitate timely referrals and clinical interventions. Optimised for low bandwidth systems, these

systems can be used in areas with limited connectivity, such as Nigeria, where internet access i s not 
the same in urban and rural areas. In the context of maternal health monitoring, this predictive ability 
can shift the paradigm of care delivery from a reactive approach – addressing complications only when 
they occur to a proactive one taking care o f the patient before her problem arises. Digital health 
technologies are being adopted in Nigeria, with rising internet penetration and government's efforts in 
the digital transformation of health services (National Information Technology Development Agency, 
2021). The National Digital Health Policy and Strategy outlines the approach to utilizing information 
and communication technology for better health service provision in the country (Federal Ministry of 
Health, 2022). The COVID -19 pandemic further highl ighted the role of digital health solutions in 
ensuring continuity of care during a public health crisis, showcasing the capabilities of technology to 
support this goal. Many of these however, are disjointed, non -integrated with predictive analytics 
functionality, and do not meet the needs of pregnant women in Nigeria (Adebayo and Adebayo, 2023). 
The existing gaps in maternal health monitoring underscore the need for developing an intelligent, 
integrated web-based system that continuously monitors maternal health during the pregnancy and 
postpartum period, utilises AI -based analysis and detection of emerging risks, and offers actionable 
suggestions and recommendations such as referral to local health care facilities. This type of system 
would help overcome t he issue of data silos, allow for ongoing tracking between appointments, and 
help healthcare professionals determine which patients need urgent care. By adopting such a system, 
built with modern web technologies and cloud infrastructure, it is possible to offer a flexible and 
scalable solution that enhances maternal health without the need for advanced mobile devices 
(Ogunyemi et al., 2023). This study aims to design and implement a Web -Based Maternal Health 
Monitoring based on AI technology to support cont inuous health monitoring during the whole 
pregnancy and postpartum period, intelligent analysis of patient -reported symptoms and test results, 
and recommend the suitable local health facilities for treatment.

1.2 Statement of the Problem 
Despite the existence of existing clinical guidelines for pre -natal care and the growing trend of using 
digital health technologies in Nigeria, maternal health outcomes are still unacceptably suboptimal. A 
predominantly episodic and reactive approach to care which is reliant on scheduled clinic visits has 
led to poor continuity of monitoring of pregnant women and women in the postpartum period, resulting 
in delayed detection of complications and preventable deaths (Okafor and Eze, 2023). A number of 
problems have been noted in maternal health monitoring as it occurs these days in Nigeria. First, 
potentially dangerous complications like pre -eclampsia, gestational diabetes and preterm labour can 
occur between scheduled visits, and if it is not monitored continuously, such  complications may 
escalate to more serious stages before they are identified, resulting in preventable maternal and infant 
death (ACOG, 2022). According to the National Population Commission (2022), Nigeria is responsible 
for about 20% of the world's mate rnal deaths, with many of them being caused by delays in the 
detection and treatment of complications. Secondly, there are often no specialists available for pregnant 
women living in rural and underserved areas to interpret test results and offer appropria te guidance. 
Maternal health data is often quite complex and may not be fully understood by general physicians 
and frontline health workers in many environments. This presents a ‘choking point' in care delivery, 
as it is easy to have complications go unrec ognized and result in severe consequences. Third, patient 
health data is often fragmented from separate systems such as hospital records, lab databases, and 
paper-based notes, making it difficult for healthcare clinicians to get a full picture of a patient's health 
status (Federal Ministry of Health, 2022). The use of manual, paper-based records is often challenging 
to access, share and analyse, resulting in a disjointed approach to healthcare delivery (Adebayo and 
Adebayo, 2023). 
Fourth, automated risk ass essment and referral systems can be missing and high -risk patients might 
not get priority care; patients may not be aware of health care facilities best suited to their needs. This 
suboptimal use of scarce clinical resources is associated with poor health outcomes and loss of scarce

health resources (Ogunyemi et al., 2023). Fifth, in Nigeria, many pregnant women do not have access 
to the smartphones required to have access to more complex mobile applications, but they have access 
to basic web browsers via s hared smartphones or community access to the internet (Adebayo and 
Adebayo, 2023).  
But current approaches also rely on high levels of technology access and literacy, making these 
solutions hard to implement in resource -limited environments. The issues identified above indicate a 
need for research to investigate the systematic incorporation of emerging web technologies and AI in 
filling gaps related to maternal health monitoring, test interpretation, and referral in Nigeria. Hence, 
the need to design and de velop an AI-assisted maternal health monitoring and referral system with a 
web platform designed to be suitable for pregnant women in Nigeria is undertaken in this study.

reported symptoms and test results and recommending the most appropriate local healthcare centre for 
treatment in Nigeria's healthcare environment.

1.7 Scope of the Study 
The study is specifically directed towards the design and realization of an AI powered Web -Based 
Maternal Health Monitoring for pregnant women in the context of the Nigerian healthcare system. The 
system is set up for maternal care from registration to pregnancy monitoring up to postpartum care. 
Through the system, patients will have the ability to safely input health data, including vital s igns, 
pregnancy-related symptoms, fatal activity, mental health evaluations, and lab test results including 
blood sugar, haemoglobin and urine protein. Patients will also have access to their health records, 
health detection trends, and customised recommendations. Using clinical criteria, an AI based analytics 
engine will be able to identify maternal complications such as pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, and 
preterm labour based on reported symptoms and clinical data. It will produce risk assessments an d 
suggest suitable actions that should follow, such as further testing, or consultation with health care 
professionals. The system will also have a ‘referral recommendation service' based on patient's 
location, risk assessment, and needed medical services.  Healthcare clinicians will be able to log 
patients into a dashboard by risk category, alert them, view patient health trends and communicate with 
them remotely. Administrators, midwives and obstetricians will be given role-based access. Technical 
solutions implemented on the front end will be with React.js and Tailwind CSS, the back end with 
Node.js and Express, the data store with PostgreSQL and the authentication and authorisation with 
JWT. The application will be available for users to access via standa rd web browsers on computers, 
tablets, and mobile devices, and will be a cloud -based web system. The research is constrained to a 
working web based minimum viable product (MVP). Does not include SMS Notification Service, 
Mobile Application for dedicated us e, Real -time IoT Data Streaming or integration with medical 
devices. These features are not studied in the current research but can be investigated in further 
enhancement.

1.8 Definition of Terms 
Maternal Health:  The health of women during pregnancy, chil dbirth, and the postpartum period, 
encompassing physical, mental, and social well-being (WHO, 2023). 
AI-Powered Maternal Health Monitoring System:  A web -based application that uses artificial 
intelligence and rule-based systems to continuously track and analyse maternal health data throughout 
pregnancy and postpartum for early detection of complications and recommendations for care. 
Predictive Risk Analytics: The use of statistical techniques, machine learning algorithms, and rule -
based systems to analyse c urrent and historical health data to predict future outcomes, enabling 
healthcare clinicians to take proactive interventions (Davidson et al., 2023). 
Pre-eclampsia: A pregnancy complication characterized by high blood pressure and signs of damage 
to another organ system, most often the liver and kidneys, and a leading cause of maternal and infant 
illness and death worldwide (ACOG, 2022). 
Gestational Diabetes: A type of diabetes that develops during pregnancy and affects how the body 
uses sugar, potentially causing complications for both mother and baby if not properly managed 
(WHO, 2023). 
Preterm Labour: Labour that begins before 37 weeks of pregnancy, leading to preterm birth and 
associated health complications for the baby (ACOG, 2022). 
Cardiotocography (CTG): A technical means of recording the fatal heartbeat and uterine contractions 
during pregnancy and labour, used to assess fatal well-being. 
Dashboard: An interactive visual interface that displays key performance indicators, metrics, and 
analytical results in a format that is easy to understand and use for supporting clinical decision-making 
(Siemens and Baker, 2012).

Rule-based System:  A type of system that uses predefined rules based on clinical guidelines and 
expert knowledge to make decisions or draw conclusions from data (Shortliffe and Buchanan, 2022). 
Referral System: A mechanism that directs patients to appropriate healthcare facilities or specialists 
based on their health needs, location, and risk level.

CHAPTER TWO: LITERATURE REVIEW 
2.1 Introduction 
This chapter presents a review of literature relevant to the design and implementation of a web-based 
maternal health monitoring and referral system. The review examines existing knowledge on maternal 
health challenges in Nigeria, digital health technologies in maternal care, AI and machine learning 
applications in maternal health, referral systems, and technological frameworks for developing web -
based health systems. The chapter is organised into thematic sections that progressively build a 
theoretical and practical foundation for the study. 
2.2 Maternal Health Challenges in Nigeria 
2.2.1 The Burden of Maternal Mortality 
In Nigeria, maternal death is a serious public health problem and Nigeria has a substantial share of 
maternal deaths worldwide. Nigeria made the 2023 progress of having a maternal mortality ratio  of 
9.99% (with almost 15 times the Sustainable Development Goal Martha target of 7 per 100,000 live 
births), meaning Nigeria accounted for about 28.5% of all maternal deaths in the world as of 2023 
(WHO, 2023). In most developed countries, one person is l ikely to die due to childbirth causes while 
in Nigeria it is 1 in 19. The high mortality rate is related to the multiplicity of factors such as poor 
health system performances, limited access to skilled health professionals, access to emergency 
obstetric services and socio-economic constraints that hinder women's capacity to access early health 
care as quickly as they need (Okafor and Eze, 2023). It is further worsened by the inadequate health 
personnel with only four health workers per 10,000 population, substantially lower than the minimum 
WHO recommended of 23 per 10,000; Nigeria's health policy targets 24 per 10,000 but has not been 
achieved. Maternal death can be attributed to the risk factors that have been documented in Nigeria: 
e.g., haemorrhage, hypertensive disorders, sepsis, unsafe abortion, obstructed labour (Okafor and Eze, 
2023). Most of these deaths are caused by eclampsia, haemorrhage and sepsis which are all preventable

when early identified. These deaths are largely preventable because of missed opportunity for referrals, 
costs and poor access to health services, especially in poor and rural areas. 
2.2.2 Limitations of Traditional Maternal Health Monitoring 
The traditional model of maternal health monitoring in Nigeria relies heavily on sched uled antenatal 
visits and periodic check-ups. Only 43% of births in Nigeria are attended by a skilled health clinician, 
and in rural areas, 60% of deliveries are conducted by Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs). This 
episodic approach has significant limit ations that become particularly pronounced in resource -
constrained settings. 
Dangerous conditions such as pre -eclampsia, gestational diabetes, and fatal distress often develop 
gradually between scheduled appointments, and without continuous monitoring, these conditions may 
progress to critical stages before detection (ACOG, 2022). Research in Lagos State recorded a maternal 
mortality ratio of 1,645 per 100,000 live births between 2015 and 2019, with unregistered pregnancies 
accounting for 72% of those deaths and almost 80% occurring outside a health facility. 
Additionally, patient health information is frequently scattered across multiple systems including 
hospital records, laboratory databases, and paper -based notes, preventing healthcare clinicians from 
seeing the complete picture of a patient's health status (Federal Ministry of Health, 2022). Healthcare 
clinicians often rely on manual, paper -based records that are difficult to access, share, and analyse, 
leading to fragmented care delivery (Adebayo and Ad ebayo, 2023). A study at Orile Agege General 
Hospital, Ile-Epo quantified care fragmentation at 88.2%, highlighting the siloed operations among 
delivery facilities.

2.2.3 Chronic Disease as an Under-Addressed Driver of Maternal Risk 
There is under addressing of chronic disease as a driver of maternal risk in Nigeria. In Nigeria, 19.1

2.4.2 Integration of Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs) 
Traditional Birth Attendants play a significant role in maternal care in Nigeria, particularly in rural

2.5 Technological Frameworks for Web-Based Health System 
2.5.1 Architecture of Web-Based Health Monitoring Systems 
The architecture of web -based health monitoring systems has evolved significantly, with modern 
systems adopting modular, scalable designs that separate frontend and backend com ponents. This 
separation of concerns facilitates development, maintenance, and scalability, which is particularly 
important in health systems that must accommodate growing user bases and evolving clinical 
requirements (Ogunyemi et al., 2023). 

===== PAGE 29 =====
 
17 
 
The typical architecture of a web-based health monitoring system includes a frontend user interface, a 
backend application server, and a database management system. The frontend, often built using 
frameworks such as React.js, handles user interactions and presentation.  The backend, implemented 
using Node.js or other server -side languages, processes requests, implements business logic, and 
manages data storage. The database stores patient data, clinical records, and system configuration. This 
three-tier architecture prov ides a solid foundation for developing scalable, maintainable health 
applications. 
The Maternal Risk Predictor project demonstrates a practical web -based health monitoring system 
using Streamlet, a Python-based framework that enables rapid development of web applications from 
machine learning models. The application uses a trained model serialised with joblib and deployed 
through a simple web interface that accepts user inputs and displays predicted risk levels. This 
demonstrates that effective health monitoring systems can be developed with a range of technologies, 
depending on the project's specific requirements and available expertise. 
2.5.2 Understanding the design of a database  
In the context of health monitoring systems, especially involving sensitive patient data, effective data 
management is crucial. The database needs to be structured to support a wide variety of data types, 
such as demographic data, clinical measurements, symptoms, and risk assessments, as well as 
providing data integrity, security , and access. (Adebayo and Adebayo, 2023). Health monitoring 
systems are often implemented on relational databases like PostgreSQL, which are known for their 
strength, data integrity capabilities, and ability to support complex queries. The schema design n eeds 
to take into account the data types and relationships to be supported by the data repository. The key 
tables that could be relevant for a maternal health monitoring system include patients, clinicians, vital 
signs, symptoms, alerts, and risk assessmen ts. The design of each table should include correct fields, 
data types, and constraints to promote quality and consistency in the data. The real -life example of 
PROMPTS illustrates the significance of customizing data management for local contexts. The 

===== PAGE 30 =====
 
18 
 
solution has been purpose built as an SMS based solution to overcome the limitations of internet 
access. This shows the practicality required in the developing world where access to the internet is not 
given. 
2.5.3 Security and privacy considerations 
Security in health information is a critical factor in building web-based health systems. Patient health 
information is confidential, and should not be accessed, disclosed, or manipulated by those who do not 
have permission. The systems will adhere to applicable data protection laws and put in place technical 
and organisational safeguards to ensure data security (Adebayo and Adebayo, 2023). Authentication 
and authorisation, data encryption, audit logging, and secure communication are some of the important 
security features in web -based health systems. JWT (JSON Web Token) authentication is a widely 
adopted authentication mechanism to validate the user identity and manage access to resources in a 
system. Role Based Access Control (RBAC) allows only restricted access to data and functions 
depending on the role. Solayo Africa encrypts user interactions and doesn't share data with third parties, 
thus outperforming a lot of local compliance standards. In Africa, with evolving regulatory 
frameworks, the handling of sensiti ve health data is still a big challenge for digital health platforms.

2.6 Theoretical Framework 
2.6.1 The Health Belief Model 
The Health Belief Model (HBM) provides a theoretical foundation for understanding why individuals 
engage in health -promoting behaviours, including the use of health monitoring systems. The model 
proposes that individuals are more likely to take health -related actions if they perceive themselves as 
susceptible to a health threat, perceive the threat as severe, believe that the benefits of action outweigh 
the costs, and have confidence in their ability to take the action (Rosenstock, 1974). 

===== PAGE 31 =====
 
19 
 
In the context of maternal health monitoring, the HBM suggests that pregnant women are more likely 
to use a monitoring system if they perceive themselves at risk of pregnancy complications, believe that 
monitoring can help prevent adverse outcomes, and feel confident in their ability to use the system. 
This framework has implications for system design, suggesting that features should be intuitive and 
accessible, that the system should clearly communicate risks and benefits, and that education and 
support should be provided to build user confidence. 
2.6.2 Technology Acceptance Model 
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) offers a framework for understanding user adoption of 
technology, proposing that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use are the primary determinants 
of technology acceptance (Davis, 1989). Perceived usefulness refers to the degree to which a user 
believes that using the technology will improve their performance, while perceived ease o f use refers 
to the degree to which the user believes that using the technology will be effort-free. 
For the proposed maternal health monitoring system, TAM suggests that adoption will be influenced 
by whether healthcare clinicians perceive the system as useful for improving patient care and whether 
they find it easy to use. Similarly, pregnant women will be more likely to use the system if they perceive 
it as useful for monitoring their health and if the system is easy to use. The success of platforms like  
Moma, which is built on WhatsApp to leverage existing user familiarity, demonstrates the importance 
of perceived ease of use.

This study's conceptual framework combines the main elements of the proposed Web-based maternal 
health m onitoring and referral system. The framework has been developed based on the literature 
review and is guided by the theories referred to above. The five components of the framework are data 
input, AI-powered analysis, risk assessment, decision support and outcomes. Electronic data input 
encompasses patient reported symptoms and test results, demographic data and medical history. Rule

Based Analysis is the use of algorithms and machine learning models to analyse patient data and 
predict potential complicatio ns, which is aided by AI. Risk Assessment determines a risk level 
according to proven clinical guidelines. Decision support contains the clinician dashboard with lists of 
patients stratified by risk, real-time alerts and recommendations for referrals. The benefits of outcomes 
are improved early identification of potential complications, timely referrals and lowered maternal

3.7 Clinical Risk-Engine Design 
Decision support is not a large language model and is not a diagnostic model - the answer is clinical 
triage. Production inference is done in typeScript. Random Forest can be trained offline with synthetic 
data the artefact is not serialised and loaded at runtime.  The in -application assistant is a keyword 
knowledge base with an escalation flag and is NOT the risk engine. 
3.7.1 Model Selection and Justification 
Maternal triage should include mixed continuous vitals and binary danger signs, safe behaviour on 
small demonstration caseloads and inspectable reasons why it is appropriate for the midwife to read 
(WHO 2016 2017). A reasonable prediction m ethod is the Random Forest, it handles non -linear 
combinations of mixed features well, is suitable for tabular data, and is not prone to overfitting.  The 
production design uses a weighted ensemble, following a forest style combination based on 
coefficients aligned to the clinical reference ranges for WHO, ACOG and NICE (ACOG, 2020; NICE, 
2019; WHO, 2016) and hard clinical overrides. No numerical value or score can "outvote" the 
emergency obstetric signs because serverless Node.js is not based on the Python runtime; therefore, 
weights are weights published for each sign, and incomplete signs are common.  We would cover up 
risk if we said that 28 "normal" readings of blood pressure 99/69 were "normal."  If no data is entered 
in a worksheet column for any vital, then it is considered "incomplete" and does not contribute 
anything.

Fourteen predictors are highly selected from maternal age, latest matching test results and recent 
symptoms. Features and production weights are provided in table 10. The fourteen inputs are not the 
same as gestational age, BMI, or parity as these are used to modify the factor list and the complication 
screens. There are narrative factors, as well as a small score boost from pre-existing hypertension and 
diabetes and a sickle-cell history.

3.7.3 Scoring, Classification, and Screens 
Partial scores are summed and clamped to the closed interval from 0 to 1. The level is HIGH if the 
score is at least 0.55, or if vaginal bleeding, reduced fatal movement, systolic pressure of 160 mmHg 
or more with diastolic pressure of 110 mmHg or more, severe headache plus blurred vision, 
hypertensive blood pressure (140/90 mmHg or more) with headache, visual change or oedema, or 
glucose of 200 mg/dL or more is present. The level is MEDIUM if the score is at least 0.28 or glucose 
is 140 mg/dL or more. Otherwise, the level is LOW (ACOG, 2020; NICE, 2019; WHO, 2017). 
When the result is not LOW, screens may trigger for pre-eclampsia, gestational diabetes, and preterm 
labour. An emergency screen is added for classic danger signs. Recommendations return suggested 
tests, actions, a care plan, and urgency (monitor, consult, or emergency). HIGH or emergency results 
are labelled RULE_BASED; o therwise, GUIDELINE_ENGINE. Supporting calculations shown to 
users include Naegele’s estimated date of delivery, gestational age in weeks, mean arterial pressure, 
body-mass index, and the WHO eight-contact ANC timetable (WHO, 2016). Figure 6 summarises the

4.10 Clinical Engine Implementation 
The maternal health monitoring system′s production inference is run through a deterministic 
TypeScript clinical engine. The engine doesn't use a dynamically loaded machine learning model at 
runtime, but instead is a weighted ensemble. It mimics a combination of features from vital signs and 
risk signs to produce real -time risk assessment close to the Random Forest approach. The feature 
coefficients and approach to weighting are explicitly guide d by published, evidence -based, clinical 
thresholds and guidelines (ACOG, 2020; NICE, 2019; WHO, 2016), and are not taken from a private, 
proprietary dataset of labelled clinical information in hospitals. This artefact could be trained offline 
for comparis on purposes or for further research, but it will not be loaded into or executed in the

production environment with Python. This architectural approach allows transparent, explainable and 
consistent inferences in front line maternal care.

4.11.1 Authentication and Authorisation 
The system is secure with an established authentication and authorisation procedure. When users log

4.11.2 Data Security Measures 
The system has a number of security measures in place to safeguard confidential patient 
information, which can be summarised as follows: 
• Password Hashing: Bcryptjs with 12 rounds of salt. Only the resulting password hash is stored 
in the User table, so no plain text is stored.  
• HTTPS / Transport Layer Security: Secure com munication is enforced in production with 
Vercel – all data in transit is encrypted. Input validation:  
• Zod schemas are used for authentication and clinical payloads which protect from injection 
attacks and malformed data submissions.  
• Registration and Password reset endpoints are protected from brute force attacks with an in -
memory rate limiter. Ensuring access to only those resources that are authorized by a user's 
role, users are controlled with Role Based Access Control (RBAC) using JSON Web Token 
roles, edge middleware and API guards.  
• Session Control: Sessions are controlled by eight -hour tokens and are logged out with 
NextAuth.  
• Password Reset: Single-use tokens are given for password reset, valid for 30 minutes. The API 
responses are maintained in generic format to avoid disclosure of information.  
• Access to Records: Only the patient's data are accessible to the patient; clinicians are only able 
to see their own caseload data.   
All these controls work together to ensure the confidentiality and integrity provisions required in 
the Nigeria Data Protection Regulation (National Information Technology Development Agency, 
2019).

5.2 Summary of the Study 
This study aimed to tackle issues that affect maternal health care in Nigeria such as incomplete 
antenatal care (ANC) contacts, delayed detection of obstetric danger signs, and deficient digital 
assistance for referral. The aim was to develop, deploy and test a Web Maternal Health Monitoring 
System (WMHS) for antenatal monitoring and offer a location -based facility refer solution as a 
decision supporting solution, in addition to a risk triage solution as per guidelines. 
The overall research protocol adopted was Design Science Research (DSR) and the development 
process was identified with the adoption of Agile Scrum Methodology and the selected System  
Development Life Cycle (SDLC) Practices. It has been developed using Next.js, React, Prisma, Neon 
PostgreSQL, NextAuth and Cloudinary and follows the WHO/ACOG/NICE guidelines. Major

features included were 8 -contact ANC monitoring, uploads of lab results a nd location-based facility 
matching of clinical-risk assessment. 
The system was subjected to structure, function and integration testing as well as testing on clinical 
situations. The 20 test cases were successfully executed along with the specified clinic al situations. 
The design and testing work entail the design of a functional digital system that would be used in an 
antenatal monitoring, risk triage and referral in a maternal health environment in Nigeria.

5.4 Contribution to Knowledge 
The contribution of this study to the design and implementation of maternal -health decisions can be 
discussed under three main research areas. 
• One, it shows an in tegrated method of antenatal monitoring and referral. The Web -based 
Maternal Health System (WMHS) integrates the collection of symptoms and tests between 
visits, clinician caseload, alerts, monitoring of the WHO 8 contacts of the antenatal care, and

72 
 
location-based referral to facilities all into one web-based system. This shows the potential for 
the functions that are usually carried out separately being connected with each other into a 
wider maternal health information system. 
• Second, the study provides a  new and explainable method of antenatal risk assessment. The 
risk engine implemented is not based on a black box model using machine learning alone, it is 
based on a risk engine that has guideline-based rules and emergency overrides based on WHO, 
ACOG and NICE guidance. Clinical observations that are not recorded in the record will be 
identified as incomplete, not normal and in fact indicating a high -risk status does not make a 
person a clinical risk; the clinical risk is indicated by the guidance. This pr ovides a design 
approach for transparency and Interpretability of Digital Antenatal decision support. 
• Thirdly, the study presents a traceable way for evaluating maternal -health software artefacts. 
Guidance-based requirements were developed based on the rel evant guidance, policies and 
antenatal scenarios and then evaluated using unit, functional and integration as well as clinical-
scenario tests. This offers a framework connectivity between identified requirements, system 
implementation and verification. 
In summary, the study is a contribution to designing a web-based integrated, explainable and testable 
maternal-health decision -support system. The contribution does not imply a clinical diagnostic 
accuracy or an improvement in maternal outcomes/effectiveness in actual clinical use of the artefact, 
only technical functionality and evaluation of the artefact. 
5.5 Limitations of the Study 
 The study has its own limitations. The study has certain limitations. 
The results of this study should be viewed in light of the following limitations: 
• First, no clinical field trial was done. The evaluation did not include the assessment of clinical 
uptake, delay in uptake or maternal health outcomes. 

===== PAGE 85 =====
 
73 
 
• Second, a test was performed on seeded demonstration accounts and synthetic records. Thus, 
the results reflect the behaviour of the system implemented in the test data and do not 
necessarily reflect the behaviour of the system in real clinical data. 
• Thirdly, there was no external labelled clinical data set to test the ri sk engine. The evaluation 
therefore demonstrates the proper application of the rules specified, and does not establish 
diagnostic accuracy. 
Lastly, there are still a few restrictions on some operational aspects. The SMS notification pathway is 
a demonstration stub, the facility directory is populated with seeded data and does not rely on a national 
database and the system is not connected to platforms like DHIS2 or electronic medical -record 
systems. WMHS is therefore designed as a decision support tool and is not to diagnose disease, be a 
substitute for health care professionals or serve for emergency transport. 
5.6 Conclusion 
In summary, this research was successful in developing, implementing and testing an online maternal 
health system that supports anten atal care monitoring, risk triage, and referral to facilities in the 
Nigerian setting. The Web -based Maternal Health Monitoring System (WMHS) developed by the 
authors incorporates a patient portal and a clinician portal; it provides a risk assessment based  on 
clinical guidelines; it enables the WHO eight-contact antenatal care (ANC) schedule to be followed; it 
generates alerts; and it ranks referrals to facilities based on location in a single, three-tier architecture. 
Unit testing of the OMHS, 20 functional test cases, integration workflow testing, and clinical scenario-
based testing demonstrated that all components of the (WMHS) function as expected on the seeded 
version of the system. Additionally, the OMHS was able to identify missing vital observations and 
classify as high risk those conditions identified as obstetric danger signs. Although this study does not 
provide evidence of clinical diagnostic accuracy nor does it demonstrate a reduction in maternal 
mortality, delay of referral, or other maternal health indicators, such findings would need to be 
substantiated through clinical and community-based evaluations.

5.7 Recommendations 
The following recommendations are based upon what was found during this study and its limitations. 
• Clinical field evaluation: The system will undergo clinical field evaluation in both primary 
healthcare settings and referral facilities. This is to determine its usability, how well it 
integrates into current work flow processes, and the practical value of the system. 
• The involvement of health care professionals such as midwives, nurses, and other relevant 
professionals will be involved with providing validation for system requirements, interface 
design, and output of risk-assessments. 
• Further validate the risk engine: Any expansion of the risk engine should be based on 
appropriately validated clinical datasets, while maintaining emergency safety rules and 
avoiding the treatment of missing clinical measurements as normal values. 
Healthcare facilities should use WMHS as a  decision-support tool rather than a replacement for 
professional clinical judgement. Emergency referral should not be delayed because of an 
application's risk classification, and women should continue to attend scheduled antenatal 
appointments and seek appropriate medical care when danger signs occur. 
5.8 Suggestions for Further Research 
Future research should extend the present Design Science Research cycle from technical 
demonstration to real-world and clinical evaluation. 
Further studies could assess us ability among antenatal clients and healthcare professionals using 
structured measures such as the System Usability Scale, task completion time and error rates. The risk 
engine could also be validated prospectively using de -identified Nigerian clinical rec ords and 
clinician-adjudicated outcomes, with particular attention to false -negative emergency classifications 
and incomplete clinical observations.

Future researchers could investigate SMS or USSD -based data capture for women with limited 
smartphone or in ternet access, as well as offline -capable functionality for areas with unreliable 
connectivity. Research could also examine interoperability between WMHS and existing health 
information and reporting platforms. 
Finally, longitudinal or quasi -experimental s tudies could investigate whether digital antenatal 
monitoring and location -based referral influence referral processes and the time taken to seek care 
when danger signs occur. If machine -learning approaches are introduced in future work, they should
